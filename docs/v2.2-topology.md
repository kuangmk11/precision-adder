# v2.2 circuit, transcribed

Read off `Schematic_precision-adder-final-v2.2.pdf` (EasyEDA, rev 2.2, 2020-10-25, drawn by
rossgrady, 508 Loop Detected) so a redraw does not mean squinting at the PDF. The design is
someone else's; this is a transcription for reference, not a claim on it.

The PDF is a vector export with no netlist in it, so nothing can convert it to a KiCad
schematic automatically — a PDF holds line segments that happen to touch, not connectivity.
What follows is the connectivity, read by eye and checked against the BOM designators.

## The idea in one line

A trimmed **±2.5 V reference pair** is the only precision source on the board. Each stage
picks one polarity of it with an SPDT, scales it down to that stage's interval with a
resistor divider, buffers it, and adds it to the stage's input at unity gain.

```
 ±12V ─→ ±5V (78L05/79L05) ─→ ±2.5V refs (OPA2196 + trimmers)
                                    │
              SPDT picks + or −  ───┤
                                    ↓
   INn ─→ buffer ─→┐        divider (fixed R + 1k trim) ─→ buffer ─→┐
                   ├──── two 10k into a summing node ───────────────┤
                   ↓                                                ↓
                 non-inverting amp, gain 2  ─────────────────────→ OUTn
   INn ─→ buffer ────────────────────────────────────────────────→ PTn
```

Gain 2 undoes the ÷2 of summing through two equal resistors, so `OUT = IN + Vbias` exactly.

## One stage, by designator

Stage 1 shown; the other three are identical but for the divider values and the op-amp
package. Four channels per stage, one quad each: `U1`–`U4` for stages 1–4.

| Block | Connectivity |
|---|---|
| Divider | `Vref1` → `R5` 10k → `VR1` 1k trimmer → `R4` 40k → GND. Wiper → `U1.1` pin 3 (+) |
| Ref buffer | `U1.1` pin 2 (−) tied to pin 1 (out), `C5` 22p across it. Unity gain |
| Damping | `U1.1` out → `R24` 619R → `C30` 330p → GND |
| Input buffer | `IN1` → `U1.2` pin 5 (+); pin 6 (−) tied to pin 7 (out) |
| Summing node | `U1.2` out → `R19` 10k → node; `U1.1` out → `R28` 10k → same node; node → `U1.3` pin 10 (+) |
| Output amp | `U1.3` pin 9 (−) ← `R21` 10k to GND, `R20` 10k feedback from pin 8. Gain 2. Pin 8 = `OUT1` |
| Passthru | `IN1` → `U1.4` pin 12 (+); pin 13 (−) tied to pin 14 (out) = `PT1` |

Per-stage dividers, all fed from ±2.5 V. The trimmer sets the final value; the fixed pair
sets the range around it.

| Stage | Target | Top R | Trim | Bottom R | Nominal |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 2 V (2 oct) | `R5` 10k | `VR1` 1k | `R4` 40k | 1.961 – 2.010 V |
| 2 | 1 V (1 oct) | `R6` 15k | `VR2` 1k | `R7` 10k | 0.962 – 1.058 V |
| 3 | 0.583333 V (5th) | `R11` 50k | `VR3` 1k | `R10` 15k | 0.568 – 0.587 V |
| 4 | 0.416667 V (4th) | `R9` 50k | `VR4` 1k | `R8` 10k | 0.410 – 0.451 V |

## Board split

Two boards. `H1`–`H4` (male) mate with `H6`, `H8`–`H10` (female), each carrying one stage's
`OUTn` / `Vrefn` / `INn` / `PTn`. `H5`/`H7` (1×3) carry `+2.5V Ref`, `−2.5V Ref`, GND to the
front board. The switches `G$1`–`G$4` live on the front board and only ever route a
reference, never a signal — which is why the switch carries no audio-path current and its
contact resistance does not matter.

Op-amp budget: 4 channels × 4 stages = 16 (`U1`–`U4`, OPA4196 quads) + 2 for the references
(`U9`, OPA2196 dual) = 18, matching the BOM.

## What v3 changes

Proposal, not yet drawn. The reference block, the power block and the summing topology carry
over unchanged — that is the proven part and it is a small fraction of the sheet.

**Drops.** `IN2`–`IN4` and `PT2`–`PT4` go, and with them the per-stage passthru buffers
(`U*.4`, 4 channels) and the input buffers on stages 2–4 (3 channels) — a cascaded stage is
fed from the previous stage's output, which is already low impedance.

**Adds.** A three-input summing amp at the front for `IN1`+`IN2`+`IN3`, and a buffer for
`SUM`. Roughly 10 channels total against v2.2's 18, so three quads become two, or two quads
plus the existing dual.

**The one new problem.** Each stage now needs *three* selectable magnitudes rather than one,
chosen by the `ON-ON-ON` selector. Three separate dividers per stage would mean twelve
trimmers. A single tapped ladder does it with one, because the taps are ratios of the same
chain and the op-amp inputs draw nothing, so selecting a tap does not disturb the others —
and one trimmer in series scales all three together.

Chains below are shown as segments from GND upward, on a 30k total, all from the same ±2.5 V:

| Stage | Segments (GND → Vref) | Taps |
|---|---|---|
| 1 `OCT` | 12k, 12k, 6k | 12k → 1.000 V (1 oct), 24k → 2.000 V (2 oct) |
| 2, 3 `3RD` | 3k, 1k, 1k, 25k | 3k → 0.250 V (m3), 4k → 0.333333 V (M3), 5k → 0.416667 V (4th) |
| 4 `5TH` | 3k, 1k, 3k, 23k | 3k → 0.250 V (m3), 4k → 0.333333 V (M3), 7k → 0.583333 V (5th) |

The trimmer sits in series at the top of the chain, so it scales every tap together and
absorbs the top segment's absolute value — meaning **only the segments below the taps need
to be precise**. The top segment can be the nearest E96 part. What matters is the ratio
between the small segments (1k, 3k, 12k), and that is where hand-matched parts earn their
keep. The top segment is drawn 0.5k light so the chain is nominal with the trimmer centred,
giving about ±1.7% either way.

At E96 the worst case on a ratio-defining segment is about 0.3%, which on a 0.25 V minor
third is 0.75 mV — **under one cent**. Ordinary 0.1% parts are comfortable and even 1%
would likely pass.
